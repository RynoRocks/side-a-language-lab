# Vision: Side A, Headstart talks back

## The one sentence
Real 1980 Japanese course tapes, made for people about to land in Japan, now talk back: the original recording plays, and in every drill pause where a learner once spoke to nobody, a live tutor listens and answers.

## Why this is a resurrection
I learned German on cassettes as a kid. You pressed rewind, then forward, then rewind again to line up the one bit you needed, and you repeated it until it stuck. Then the tape left a pause for you to answer, and nobody answered.

These Japanese tapes were recorded in 1980 for people about to land in Japan. They're public domain now, and they're still good. What killed them was the silence in the pause.

People are still landing in Japan every week to work and study, and from April 2027 the new worker visa asks for A1 Japanese before work starts. So we're bringing the tapes back, and this time the pause talks back.

## Who it is for
English speakers about to move to Japan for work or study, starting from zero (aiming at A1): interns, new hires, workers from English-speaking countries such as the Philippines. They have 10 minutes, a phone and earphones, and a date they land. First real users: interns joining Aoyama Pro in Tokyo.

## The one thing it must do brilliantly
The moment the tape answers. The 1980 speaker says a line, the pause comes, the learner speaks Japanese, and within a second or two a tutor replies to what they actually said, then the tape rolls on. If a change does not make that moment faster, clearer or more surprising, do not make it.

## What is in (the whole product)
1. The shelf: four tapes from the DLI Japanese Headstart course (Defense Language Institute, 1980, public domain), using the original audio. Which four modules is decided after listening (start from Modules 2A to 3B).
2. The deck: the tape slides in, the reels turn, the counter runs. Play, pause, rewind and fast-forward are real controls on real audio: tap rewind to hear the last line again, hold it to go further back.
3. The pause listens: drill pauses in the original recording are found automatically. When one starts, the mic opens and a REC light shows.
4. The tape talks back: a live tutor voice answers in the pause, in simple English with the Japanese it is teaching. It confirms, corrects one thing, or asks one short follow-up, then hands back to the tape. The tutor is a new voice, clearly not the original speakers. For a beat as it speaks, the hiss and wow shift, as if something is in the tape.
5. The J-card: at the end of a tape, a card lists what the learner said, with one fix each, and the tape's track list.

## What is out (do not build, do not suggest)
- Login, accounts, payments, databases. Progress lives in the browser only.
- More than four tapes. Other target languages. Explanations in languages other than English (later).
- The teacher's console for classrooms. It is the next step in the pitch, not a feature to build now.
- Cloning or imitating the voices of the original speakers.
- Uploading your own tapes.
- Mixtapes, music, playlists.
- Gamification: points, streaks, badges, leaderboards, confetti.
- Claims that the app helps anyone pass a test or get a visa or a job.
- Upsells, pop-ups, banners, sign-ups, "share your score".
- Any code, assets, agents or data from LoLA, LokaLingo, Aoyama Pro or other existing products.
- Extra sections that are not part of the drill (testimonials, stats, FAQ, blog).

## How to judge a change
Before proposing or making a change, answer these three questions in one line each:
1. Which item on the "in" list does it improve?
2. What would a learner notice?
3. What can be removed instead?

Removing or simplifying something is a valid change, and often the best one. Never add a feature only to show progress. When a spec is done and its tests pass, stop and say so.

## Source material rules
- Audio comes only from Internet Archive items marked Public Domain (DLI Japanese Headstart, 1980). Keep the item identifiers in `content/SOURCES.md`.
- Credit the Defense Language Institute on the shelf and in the README.
- Never trim the original audio in a way that changes what the speaker says.

## Quality bar
- Works on a phone at 390px wide, and with the keyboard alone.
- Text contrast passes WCAG AA.
- The tutor's reply starts within 2 seconds of the learner finishing.
- Every task ends with a passing build. A change that breaks an earlier feature is a failure, even if the new part works.
- English UI. Plain words. No exclamation marks in UI copy.

## Done means
A stranger on a phone picks a tape, hears the 1980 recording, answers in a pause, hears the tape talk back, rewinds a line once, and reads their J-card, with no instructions, in under 10 minutes.
